Deployment bugs found and fixed

In the order they were found, each blocking real progress until fixed.

1

Out-of-distribution start

Deployment began already at the pick target (dist≈0); training always reset to one fixed home pose ~0.84 m from target, so the policy had zero experience near-zero. Fixed by starting from real_home_position.

2

Step-budget mismatch

MAX_STEPS was 30–80, calibrated to an older checkpoint's 50-step episodes. This checkpoint trained with max_episode_steps=500. Raised to match.

3

Gripper missing from the real URDF

The driver's stock launch has no gripper model — EE pose was approximated at tool0 (~0.17 m fixed-offset guess) instead of the trained reference point. Fixed with a combined arm+gripper URDF, real TF frames wired in.

4

Joint-state merger silently zeroing the arm

The first TF fix used joint_state_publisher's merge, which zeroed all 6 arm joints in its output — corrupting every distance computed downstream. Replaced with a minimal custom merger, unit-verified before redeploying.

5

Target measured with bug 4 still active

The first re-measurement of the pick target used the broken merger and was garbage (run 13, discarded). Re-measured after the fix; cross-checked against the original constant — consistent to within the expected gripper-length offset.

05

Root cause candidate

Confirmed in source, corroborated live

Self-collision detection is hardcoded off in the training simulator — self_collision=False, set at URDF-import time in ur5e_grasp_env.py. Only a simplified table-collision proxy exists; the arm and gripper were never able to intersect in ~630,000 training steps, so the policy never had the opportunity to learn that they could.

Run 16's real, visually-confirmed self-collision landed in the same 0.7–0.9 m band where most trials independently lose coherence — physical corroboration, not just a code-reading inference.

06

Open questions

  • Whether the step-29 clustering (runs 14, 15) is a genuine step-count-linked cause or coincidence — two data points isn't enough to tell.
  • Most link-drop stops were never individually root-caused; one was confirmed as a Safety Control Board comms fault (C4A3) and is suspected to explain the rest, since drops occurred regardless of what motion was commanded — including a plain home-return move.
  • No live perception was in the loop this session — the target was a fixed measured constant throughout, not camera-detected. A separate sim-to-real gap, unaddressed here.
